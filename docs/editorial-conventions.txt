PARKWAY UNFOLDED — EDITORIAL CONVENTIONS
Audience: everyday New Jersey drivers.
Use exits, ramps, connecting roads, drawings, curves and vehicles a day.
Explain each comparison beside its number. Keep technical formulas and road
identifiers in optional data notes. A drawing can show a whole ramp, a fork or
part of a shared road; do not turn the drawing count into a count of physical
ramps. Traffic counts are vehicles, not people or live congestion. Missing data
stays unavailable. An estimated curve is not an engineering or safety finding.
Curiosity should lead to a specific, supported view. Avoid claims about why a
road was designed a certain way unless a reliable source supports them.

Examples of revised language:
Road-section AADT -> vehicles a day, on average.
Selected source path -> ramp drawing or the road shown.
Principal-axis bounding box -> a box around the shape.
Charged movement -> where you pay.
Source record and verification -> about this drawing: data and notes.

Suggested upkeep for future editions:
Retain a visible year for every layer. Review published toll schedules when
rates change, traffic when a newer annual release is verified, and road shapes
when a new mapping source has been reviewed. Preserve previous editions and
source definitions. The page does not promise a live feed or automatic updates.

Visual convention, October 23, 2026: flat white/charcoal surfaces, Arial/Helvetica,
14 px minimum supporting UI type, neutral selected controls, meaningful road/data
colors, no decorative card stripes, shadows, gradients, blur, or reveal effects.
Top 10s keeps its trophy icon and gold emphasis. Preserve the actual road geometry.
